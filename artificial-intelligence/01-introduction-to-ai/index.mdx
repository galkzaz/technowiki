# What Is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science concerned with building computer systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, understanding language, recognizing images, making decisions, and generating new content.

In simple terms:

Artificial Intelligence is about making machines capable of performing intelligent tasks by processing information, learning from experience, reasoning about problems, and taking appropriate actions.

For example:

- A chess program analyzes possible moves and selects a strategy.
- A self-driving car recognizes roads, pedestrians, and traffic signs.
- A recommendation system suggests movies based on your interests.
- A virtual assistant understands questions and responds in natural language.
- ChatGPT generates text, explains concepts, and writes computer programs.

AI is a multidisciplinary field within computer science that boldly strives to create systems capable of emulating and surpassing human-level intelligence.

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 What problem does AI solve?

Traditional computer programs follow instructions explicitly written by programmers.

Consider a simple spam detection program.

Traditional programming:

A programmer defines rules:

```
if (email.contains("win money")) {
    return SPAM;
}

if (email.contains("claim your prize")) {
    return SPAM;
}

return NOT_SPAM;
```

This approach works when the programmer can anticipate the relevant conditions.

However, real-world problems are often more complicated:

- Spam messages continuously change.
- Images contain objects in different positions and lighting conditions.
- Human language has ambiguity and context.
- Driving requires responding to unpredictable situations.

It is difficult to manually define rules for every possible situation.

AI-based approach:

Instead of explicitly programming every rule, we provide an AI system with examples and allow it to learn patterns.

The important distinction is that machine learning allows a system to learn patterns from data instead of relying entirely on manually written rules.

Not all AI uses machine learning, though. Some AI systems use explicitly programmed rules, logic, and search algorithms.

## 2. Key Concepts

To understand AI properly, you need to understand its main concepts and how they relate to each other.

### 2.1 Artificial Intelligence vs. Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning vs. Generative AI

These terms are closely related, but they do not mean the same thing.

[AI relation to Generative Models subset diagram](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/a1gqEZKtO9xrbnGKB43xJUwDx_19sIvc4tRq3_2MGBq7V16g-JgFmqHV2NTE3GwIG-ErT47BdYUCbphwc_GNigyISPyUXpvEzmG7hRpr4l0dhgODCoxY1ZlZRcWfusn-tqdFArIZITbFNu0aqC5RneScuQQy7x4ulj7qeBoF3vSeuEBvjlMyd9fE79_oWxhi?purpose=inline)

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The broad field of creating intelligent computer systems.

Examples:

- Expert systems.
- Search algorithms.
- Planning systems.
- Machine learning systems.
- Robotics.

Machine Learning (ML)

A subfield of AI that focuses on algorithms that learn patterns from data and use those patterns to make predictions or decisions.

Examples:

- Spam classification.
- House price prediction.
- Customer segmentation.

Deep Learning (DL)

A subfield of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with multiple layers to learn complex patterns.

Examples:

- Image recognition.
- Speech recognition.
- Natural language processing.
- Autonomous driving perception.

Generative AI (GenAI)

A category of AI systems designed to generate new content based on patterns learned from training data.

Examples:

- Text generation using Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Image generation.
- Audio generation.
- Video generation.

The relationship can be summarized as:

```
Artificial Intelligence (AI)
│
├── Rule-Based AI
│   ├── Expert Systems
│   ├── Logic and Reasoning
│   └── Search and Planning
│
└── Machine Learning (ML)
    │
    ├── Traditional Machine Learning
    │   ├── Decision Trees
    │   ├── Random Forests
    │   └── Support Vector Machines
    │
    └── Deep Learning (DL)
        │
        ├── Convolutional Neural Networks
        ├── Recurrent Neural Networks
        ├── Transformers
        │
        └── Generative AI
            ├── Large Language Models
            ├── Diffusion Models
            └── Generative Audio Models
```

Note: This is a simplified conceptual hierarchy. Generative AI is not limited to deep learning, although most modern generative AI systems use it.

### 2.3 Types of AI based on capabilities

AI can be classified according to the level of intelligence it is intended to achieve.

1\. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI)

Also called Weak AI.

Designed to perform specific tasks.

Examples:

- Chess-playing systems.
- Recommendation engines.
- Facial recognition.
- Voice assistants.
- Current generative AI applications.

2\. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)

A hypothetical form of AI that would have broad intellectual capabilities comparable to human capabilities across different domains.

It would be able to learn and solve a wide variety of unfamiliar problems without being restricted to a narrow set of tasks.

3\. Artificial Superintelligence (ASI)

A hypothetical form of AI whose intellectual capabilities would substantially exceed those of humans across most domains.

ANI — Narrow AI

Exists today

# Specific tasks

Specialized capabilities within particular domains

AGI — General AI

Hypothetical

# Broad capabilities

Human-level general intellectual abilities

ASI — Superintelligence

Hypothetical

# Beyond human capabilities

Superior intellectual capabilities across most domains

Important: More advanced generative AI does not automatically mean AGI has been achieved.

### 2.4 Types of Machine Learning

Machine learning is commonly divided into four major learning approaches.

| Approach                 | How it learns                                                 | Example              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Supervised Learning      | Learns from labeled examples.                                 | Spam detection       |
| Unsupervised Learning    | Finds patterns in unlabeled data.                             | Customer clustering  |
| Semi-Supervised Learning | Uses a small labeled dataset with a larger unlabeled dataset. | Image classification |
| Reinforcement Learning   | Learns through actions and rewards.                           | Game-playing agents  |

A simple example of each:

- Supervised: Give a model thousands of emails labeled as spam or legitimate.
- Unsupervised: Give a model customer purchasing data and let it discover customer groups.
- Semi-supervised: Give a model 1,000 labeled images and 100,000 unlabeled images.
- Reinforcement: Let an agent play a game and reward it for winning.

We will explore these approaches in detail when studying Machine Learning.

## 3. How Artificial Intelligence Works

There is no single architecture or algorithm that describes every AI system.

However, a typical machine learning-based AI system follows a common workflow.

### 3.1 The AI development lifecycle

1. Problem Definition

   Identify the problem the AI system should solve.

   Example: Predict whether an email is spam.
2. Data Collection

   Collect relevant data from databases, files, APIs, sensors, or other sources.

   Example: Collect thousands of emails.
3. Data Preparation

   Clean, transform, normalize, and organize the data.

   Example: Extract text and remove irrelevant information.
4. Model Selection

   Choose an appropriate machine learning algorithm or neural network architecture.

   Example: Select a classification model.
5. Model Training

   Feed training data into the algorithm and adjust its parameters to learn patterns.

   Example: Learn which words and patterns are associated with spam.
6. Model Evaluation

   Test the trained model using data it has not seen during training.

   Example: Measure classification accuracy, precision, and recall.
7. Deployment

   Make the trained model available through an application, API, or embedded system.

   Example: Integrate the model into an email service.
8. Monitoring and Improvement

   Monitor performance, detect data changes, and retrain when necessary.

   Example: Update the model when spam techniques change.

### 3.2 Understanding the model mathematically

A machine learning model can be represented as a mathematical function:

$$
\hat{y}=f(x;\theta)
$$

Where:

- $x$: Input data.
- $f$: Model function.
- $\theta$: Model parameters.
- $\hat{y}$: Predicted output.

During training, the model learns suitable values for its parameters.

The objective is usually to minimize a loss function:

$$ 
\theta^\*=\arg\min\_{\theta} L(f(x;\theta),y)
$$

Where:

- $y$: Expected output.
- $L$: Loss function.
- $\theta^\*$: Optimized model parameters.

In other words, the model repeatedly adjusts its internal parameters to make better predictions.

## 4. Practical Example: Building an AI Spam Classifier

Let's use a simple example to understand the difference between traditional programming and machine learning.

Problem: Determine whether an email is spam or legitimate.

### Step 1: Collect training data

Suppose we have the following dataset:

| Email content                     | Label      |
| --------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Win a free iPhone now!            | Spam       |
| Meeting scheduled for tomorrow    | Legitimate |
| Claim your cash prize             | Spam       |
| Please review the attached report | Legitimate |
| Congratulations, you won money    | Spam       |

The label is the expected classification.

### Step 2: Extract features

The system converts email content into numerical information that a model can process.

For a simplified example:

| Feature            | Meaning                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Contains "free"    | 1 if present, otherwise 0 |
| Contains "prize"   | 1 if present, otherwise 0 |
| Contains "meeting" | 1 if present, otherwise 0 |
| Number of links    | Count of links in email   |

The model receives numerical input instead of directly reasoning over the original email text.

### Step 3: Train the model

The learning algorithm examines the examples and adjusts its parameters to identify patterns associated with spam.

For illustration, imagine the model learns that:

- Messages containing "prize" are more likely to be spam.
- Messages containing "meeting" are more likely to be legitimate.

These are learned associations, not guaranteed rules.

### Step 4: Make a prediction

Now we provide a new email:

```
Congratulations! Claim your free prize now!
```

The trained model processes the email and produces a prediction.

Example inference

Congratulations! Claim your free prize now!

Predicted class

SPAM

Illustrative result from a hypothetical trained classifier, not a real model evaluation.

### Step 5: Deploy the model

We can expose the trained model through a REST API.

For example, a Java Spring Boot application could call a Python-based machine learning service:

```
                Email Client
                     |
                     v
             Spring Boot Backend
                     |
                     v
              REST API Request
                     |
                     v
          Python ML Inference Service
                     |
                     v
              Trained ML Model
                     |
                     v
             Spam Classification
                     |
                     v
              API Response
```

This demonstrates how AI can become part of a traditional software architecture.

### 5.1 AI approaches and techniques

Historically, AI has used several approaches:

- Symbolic AI: Uses explicit symbols, facts, logical rules, and reasoning.
- Search and Optimization: Explores possible solutions to find suitable or optimal outcomes.
- Probabilistic AI: Uses probability theory to reason under uncertainty.
- Machine Learning: Learns patterns from data.
- Neural Networks: Uses interconnected computational units inspired by biological neurons.
- Evolutionary Computing: Uses ideas inspired by natural evolution to optimize solutions.

These approaches can also be combined in hybrid AI systems.

## 7. Advantages and Limitations of AI

### Advantages

1. Automation: Reduces repetitive manual work.
2. Speed: Processes large datasets quickly.
3. Pattern recognition: Identifies complex patterns in data.
4. Prediction: Supports forecasting and decision-making.
5. Scalability: Can serve many users and process large volumes of information.
6. Personalization: Adapts recommendations and experiences to individual users.

### Limitations

1. Data dependency: Many AI systems require large amounts of high-quality data.
2. Bias: Models can learn biases present in their training data.
3. Hallucinations: Generative AI can produce convincing but incorrect information.
4. Explainability: Complex models can be difficult to interpret.
5. Computational cost: Training and operating advanced models can be expensive.
6. Security: AI systems can be vulnerable to adversarial inputs and data poisoning.
7. Privacy: Training and inference may involve sensitive information.
8. Reliability: AI predictions are not guaranteed to be correct.

AI should therefore be treated as a tool that supports human decision-making, not automatically as an authoritative source of truth.

## 8. How AI Relates to Other Computer Science Fields

AI does not exist independently of the rest of computer science. It builds on several fundamental disciplines.

Artificial Intelligence

Mathematics

Linear algebra, calculus, probability, statistics

Algorithms & Data Structures

Search, optimization, graphs, complexity

Programming

Python, Java, C++, software engineering

Computer Architecture

CPU, GPU, memory, parallel processing

Databases & Data Engineering

Data storage, processing, pipelines

Distributed Systems

Parallel training, distributed inference, scalability

Some particularly important relationships:

- Mathematics: Machine learning relies heavily on linear algebra, calculus, probability, and statistics.
- Algorithms: Search algorithms, optimization algorithms, and graph algorithms are fundamental to many AI techniques.
- Computer architecture: GPUs and parallel computing make large-scale neural network training practical.
- Software engineering: AI models must be integrated into reliable, maintainable applications.
- Distributed systems: Large AI workloads often require multiple machines working together.

## 9. Suggested Learning Sequence

If your goal is to become an AI/ML engineer, it is useful to study AI as a collection of connected disciplines rather than starting directly with deep learning or generative AI.

A recommended sequence:

1. Computer Science Foundations

   Programming, algorithms, data structures, complexity analysis.
2. Mathematics for AI

   Linear algebra, calculus, probability, statistics, optimization.
3. Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals

   AI history, intelligent agents, search, knowledge representation, reasoning, planning.
4. Machine Learning

   Supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, evaluation.
5. Deep Learning

   Neural networks, backpropagation, CNNs, RNNs, Transformers.
6. Specialized AI Domains

   NLP, computer vision, speech processing, robotics.
7. Generative AI and LLMs

   Transformers, tokenization, embeddings, attention, RAG, fine-tuning, agents.
8. AI Engineering and MLOps

   Model deployment, APIs, Docker, Kubernetes, monitoring, model lifecycle management.

## 10. Summary

Artificial Intelligence is the broad discipline of creating computer systems capable of performing tasks associated with intelligence.

The most important concepts to remember:

- AI is the broad field.
- Machine Learning is a subfield of AI that learns from data.
- Deep Learning is a subfield of ML based on multi-layer neural networks.
- Generative AI creates new content.
- Training is the process of learning model parameters.
- Inference is the process of using a trained model.
- AI combines mathematics, algorithms, programming, data, and computing infrastructure.

The central idea: Instead of manually defining every possible solution, AI allows computers to learn patterns, reason about problems, and produce useful results.
