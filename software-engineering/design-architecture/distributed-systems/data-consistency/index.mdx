## Overview

Distributed systems operate in unreliable environments where:
- networks fail
- nodes crash
- requests timeout
- messages are duplicated
- services become unavailable

Reliability engineering focuses on designing systems that continue operating correctly despite these failures.

---

# Goals of Reliability

A reliable distributed system aims to:

- tolerate failures gracefully
- recover automatically
- minimize downtime
- preserve correctness
- avoid cascading failures
- maintain acceptable performance under stress

---

# Why Reliability Matters

Modern systems are:
- distributed across regions
- composed of many services
- dependent on networks
- asynchronous
- highly concurrent

This creates unavoidable failure scenarios such as:
- partial failures
- retry storms
- inconsistent replicas
- duplicate event delivery
- unavailable dependencies

Reliability mechanisms reduce the impact of these failures.

---

# Reliability vs Availability vs Consistency

## Reliability
Ability of the system to continue functioning correctly over time.

## Availability
Probability that the system is operational and reachable.

## Consistency
Guarantee that clients observe correct and predictable data state.

These concepts are related but distinct.

# Common Reliability Challenges

## Partial Failures

Some components fail while others continue operating.

---

## Network Partitions

Nodes become unable to communicate.

---

## Retry Amplification

Retries can overload already failing systems.

---

## Cascading Failures

Failure in one service propagates through dependencies.

---

## Duplicate Processing

Repeated operations may corrupt state.

# Reliability Tradeoffs

Improving reliability often introduces tradeoffs:

| Concern | Tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Strong consistency | Higher latency |
| Retries | Increased load |
| Replication | Synchronization overhead |
| Redundancy | Higher infrastructure cost |
| Fault isolation | System complexity |

---

# Reliability Principles

## Design for Failure

Failures are inevitable in distributed systems.

---

## Fail Fast

Detect problems early and avoid hidden corruption.

---

## Isolate Failures

Prevent one component failure from spreading.

---

## Prefer Graceful Degradation

Partial functionality is often better than total outage.

---

## Build Observability Into the System

Failures must be diagnosable.
